01 / The headline

Three things to know

The campaign-level read is one story. The site-level read is a much bigger one. Total weekly sales lifted ~50% off the recent price change, and the lift is being delivered through Direct and Organic. The shape of that uplift is consistent with Meta's broad reach campaigns priming the audience, even though last-click attribution credits the conversion elsewhere.

Total site sales · weekly +50%

1,255 sales last week, 1,218 the week before — versus 720 to 820 across the four prior weeks. The price change opened the door. The volume to walk through it had to come from somewhere.

Where the lift is landing +62%

Direct sales rose from 247 to 416. Google organic from 229 to 349. Both are textbook assist channels — people who saw, remembered, and came back to convert at the new price.

Meta reach over the same period 3×

Meta reach climbed from 90K to 279K weekly across the last six weeks. That broad-reach exposure is the most plausible source of the priming behind the Direct and Organic lift.

02 / The bigger picture

A step-change in weekly sales, and where it came from

For four weeks, total site sales sat between 720 and 820. Then they jumped to 1,218 and held at 1,255. That is a 50% increase in weekly conversion volume, and it lines up with the recent price change. The interesting question is which channels delivered the lift — and whether Meta's broad reach is part of the answer even if last-click does not say so.

The pattern in the data

Meta reach tripled over the last six weeks, climbing steadily from 90K to 279K weekly impressions reached. Total site sales sat flat through that scale-up — until the price change. Then they stepped up by 50% and held.

The lift did not arrive evenly. Direct sales nearly doubled (208 → 416). Google organic rose 41% (247 → 349). Refer a Friend more than doubled (125 → 273). Meta paid social, in last-click GA terms, contributed 3 to 13 conversions across the same window.

A reasonable read

The price change is the trigger. But triggers need an audience that already knows the brand and is open to switching. That audience does not appear from nowhere.

Meta's broad reach campaigns, scaling for six weeks ahead of the price change, are the most plausible source of the warmed-up audience now converting via Direct and Organic. The brand sat in front of more people. The price change gave those people a reason to act. The conversion landed last-click on whichever channel they used to come back.

07 / Next week

What we are doing about it

  1. Protect the broad reach budget

    Meta reach has tripled over six weeks. Sales have stepped up 50%. Even if last-click does not credit Meta, the timing and shape of the lift fits the assist pattern. Hold or grow upper-funnel reach spend rather than reallocating it to last-click optimised tactics.

  2. Refresh the Free 3 video creative

    Cost per Sale doubled while reach climbed 48% week on week. Classic creative fatigue. Brief a new "Free 3 General" variant against the 35 to 44 female cohort and rotate out the lowest performer. Avoid further budget into a tiring asset.

  3. Concentrate EV Plan spend on 35–54

    35 to 44 and 45 to 54 produced 921 of last week's 1,502 LPVs (61%) at the campaign's strongest cost per LPV. Tighten age targeting on the next ad set rotation and let the 55+ bracket continue earning lower-cost reach without drawing budget.

  4. Test a Refer a Friend retargeting layer in Meta

    RAF more than doubled to 273 conversions last week and converts at 17%. A Meta retargeting ad serving the referral offer to people who clicked an OVO ad but did not convert is the highest-leverage single test we are not yet running.
